Who we are
Fallow land is not wasteland.
It is land resting — recovering,
holding memory, waiting.

We believe every degraded acre
still knows what it once was.
Earth Fallow Alliance works at the intersection of ecology and people. We plant trees where forests once stood, carry rural voices into policy rooms that rarely hear them, rethink how communities handle what they discard, and give young people the tools to think like stewards — not just consumers.
Our four pillars

What we
stand for.

04

Reforestation & Land Restoration
Returning degraded land to ecological life — through community-led planting, soil regeneration, and long-term stewardship.

Bringing community evidence into national and regional climate conversations. The people closest to the land should be closest to the table.

Waste Reduction & Circular Economy

A fellowship and ambassador programme for the next generation — building the environmental leaders this continent needs.

Programs

Rooted in
real places.

Every programme we run is co-designed with the communities it serves. We do not arrive with blueprints — we arrive with questions. Everything that follows is built from the answers.

01
Restoration
The Fallow Fields Initiative
Community-led tree planting and soil regeneration in degraded agricultural zones. We train local planters, establish seed banks, and stay for the long run.
02
Advocacy
The Community Climate Desk
Policy briefs, public campaigns, and direct community representation at national and regional environmental bodies.
03
Circular Economy
Zero-to-Landfill Pilots
Working with local markets and producers to test waste-free supply chains, composting networks, and upcycling hubs.
04
Education
Green Futures Fellowship
An annual fellowship for 18–25 year olds to design and implement a local environmental project — with full mentorship and funding.
05
Community
Alliance Ambassadors
A volunteer network of local champions embedding environmental practice into markets, schools, and community gatherings.
06
Research
The Land Memory Project
Documenting ecological change at a hyper-local level — partnering universities with elders who remember what the land once looked like.
